REFINED IDEAS:
1. REVERSER — Signal Inversion Toggle
My verdict: Good idea, but treat it as a diagnostic tool, not a live trading feature.

This is a well-known technique in quantitative trading. The logic is sound — if a strategy has positive expectancy in reverse, it means the original signals have real predictive power, just pointed the wrong way. Useful for:

Confirming your signal logic actually captures something real (random signals reversed are still random)
Quickly testing contrarian hypotheses without rebuilding the EA
The risk: you still have the same entry timing, same spread costs, same SL distances. Reversing a trend-following signal in a strongly trending market will bleed money fast. So it should live behind a clear input toggle that defaults to off, with a warning log when active. Worth implementing — it's a few lines of code.

2. ADAPTIVE — Trade Journal with Stored Conditions
My verdict: Excellent idea. This is what separates serious traders from guessers.

The professional term for what you're describing is MAE/MFE tracking — Maximum Adverse Excursion and Maximum Favourable Excursion. These two numbers per trade are more valuable than almost anything else:

MFE (how far in profit before close): If your MFE is consistently 2× your actual profit, your TP is too tight or your trail is cutting you out early
MAE (how far against you before close): If your MAE is consistently just below your SL, your SL placement is right. If trades are closing at full SL but MAE was only 30% of SL, you're being stopped out on noise
For storage in MT5 — CSV files are the right call. No DLL required, human-readable, importable into Excel for analysis, and MT5's file API (FileOpen, FileWrite) handles them natively. JSON needs manual string-building in MQL5 and is harder to analyze. The CSV will sit in MT5's Files/ folder.

What I would store beyond what you listed: the full signal score at entry (OF score, VP bias, ORB state, confluence count), the time of day, spread at entry, and whether it was a REVERSER trade or normal. Definitely implementing this.

3. THE GUARDIAN — Phantom Trade Layer
My verdict: The concept is right but the execution needs adjustment to be statistically meaningful.

What you're describing is real and used professionally — it's called regime detection or walk-forward validation. The idea that you test the waters with phantom trades before committing real money is sound. Here's where I'd push back:

The 2-trade problem: Looking at 2 phantom trades at a time tells you almost nothing statistically. You could flip a fair coin twice and get heads-heads. A strategy that wins 60% of the time will still lose 2 in a row 16% of the time. Acting on 2 trades would cause the EA to wrongly pause real trading very often.

What I'd actually build: Track a rolling window of the last 10-20 phantom trades. Calculate:

Rolling win rate
Rolling R:R achieved
Which direction (normal vs reversed) is winning
Only gate real trading when the rolling phantom win rate drops below a meaningful threshold (e.g. below 35% over the last 15 trades — meaning the strategy is genuinely broken in current conditions, not just having a bad run). Resume when win rate recovers above the threshold.

The "place both directions simultaneously" idea is smart for detecting which side the market favours right now — this is genuinely useful. Worth implementing with the rolling window adjustment.

4. LIVE TRADE MANAGER — Intelligent Position Exit
My verdict: The most impactful of the four features. This is where real edge lives.

What you're describing is the single biggest difference between amateur and professional exit management. The problem you've identified is real and documented:

Trade goes 80% to TP, reverses, hits SL — you gave back 1.8R when you could have taken 0.8R
Trade dips 40% toward SL, then runs to TP — amateur panics and closes, pro holds
The professional rule is: don't exit because the price moved, exit because the signal has changed.

Here's how I would implement it concretely, using the signal stack we already have:

When a trade is open and price has reached at least 50% of the way to TP (meaning there's real profit there), activate enhanced monitoring
If price then reverses back toward entry, re-run the full EvaluateEntry() signal check on each new bar
If the signal flips to the opposite direction with confluence ≥ threshold → this means the market has genuinely changed, close for profit now
If the signal is neutral or still confirms original direction → hold, the reversal is noise
When a trade is at a loss: do not interfere. That's what the SL is for. The only exception is if a new strong signal fires in the opposite direction with high confluence — at that point, close and reverse if Guardian also confirms
The MFE data from the Adaptive module feeds directly into this: over time the EA learns what "normal" retracement looks like for each signal type, and can calibrate how much drawback to tolerate before calling the trade invalidated.

WHAT WAS IMPLEMENTED:
8 files changed total — 3 new modules created, 5 existing files updated.

1. REVERSER (ScalperEA.mq5)
Two input toggles: InpReverser (always reverse, manual) and InpAutoReverse (let Guardian decide).

When active, the flip is done correctly — same ATR-based distances, same R:R, just mirrored:

origDir is saved before the flip so Guardian phantom spawning always uses the natural signal for unbiased comparison
Entry recalculated: ASK for longs, BID for shorts
SL and TP distances stay identical, just moved to the other side
The reason log shows "REVERSED" so you can see it in MT5's journal
2. Adaptive Journal (AdaptiveJournal.mqh)
Writes a CSV to MT5/MQL5/Files/ScalperEA_Journal.csv. For every real trade it captures at entry: OF score, VP bias, ORB signal, absorption, exhaustion, delta divergence, confluence, ATR, spread, GMT hour, whether the REVERSER was active.

While the trade is open, each bar it updates:

MFE — how far price moved in your favour before closing
MAE — how far against you it went before closing
On close it writes everything including: exit time, exit price, P&L, exit reason (SL/TP/EA-close), MAE and MFE in price and in R-multiples, and a ReversalWouldWin flag (YES if the trade lost and reversal would have captured profit instead).

3. Guardian — Phantom Trade Layer (Guardian.mqh)
Runs GRD_UpdateOnTick() on every tick before the bar gate — this is the 24/7 monitoring you asked for.

On each valid signal, it spawns two imaginary trades — one NORMAL, one REVERSED — with the same SL/TP distances, checking bid/ask tick by tick until each leg hits its SL or TP. It never places real orders; it tracks everything in memory.

Results go into a rolling window of the last 20 completed pairs. The decisions it makes:

GRD_CanTrade() = false → both directions win less than 35% → market is too noisy, real trading paused
GRD_PreferReversed() = true → reversed side winning 10%+ more than normal → triggers auto-reversal (if InpAutoReverse = true)
Requires at least 5 completed phantom pairs before gating anything
All phantom results are also logged to ScalperEA_Guardian.csv so you can review the data.

4. Live Trade Manager (LiveTradeManager.mqh)
Runs each new bar alongside the existing BE/trail logic, never against it.

The rule: do not close a trade at a loss — SL handles that. It only acts when price is in profit.

Activation: once MFE reaches 50% of the TP1 distance (configurable via InpLTM_MFEThresh), enhanced monitoring switches on. From that point:

Condition	Action
Signal flipped (confluence ≥ 2) + price retraced 80%+ of MFE	Close — clear reversal confirmed
Price retraced 80%+ of MFE + MFE was ≥ 80% of TP1 distance	Close — deep gain at serious risk
Signal flipped but price only retraced lightly	Hold — too early to call
Signal still confirms original direction	Hold — trade is still valid
Price at a loss	Ignore — SL will handle it

### Summary of Risk Management Protocols in Trading Environments

This video provides an in-depth exploration of **risk management protocols** tailored to three distinct trading environments: **hedge funds, proprietary (prop) firms, and personal accounts**, with additional insights into **competition trading** frameworks. The presenter emphasizes that **risk management—not raw returns or technical strategies—is the core differentiator of professional trading**.

---

### Hedge Fund Risk Management

**Goal:**  
- Maximize **sustainable risk-adjusted compounding** over time  
- Achieve the highest possible **long-term return for an acceptable risk level and survivability**  
- Comply with legal and regulatory frameworks (although hedge funds sometimes face fines for market manipulation but still profit substantially)  

**Key Characteristics:**  
- **Diversification by strategy**: global macro, event-driven, quantitative, trend following, multi-strategy blends, arbitrage, HFT, etc.  
- Focus on **asymmetric returns**: minimize risk while maximizing potential upside  
- Structured corporate entity with specialized departments: portfolio management, research and analytics, trading execution, risk profiling, operations, auditing, and legal compliance  
- **Risk-first protocol**: hedge funds prioritize risk control before targeting returns  

**Performance Context:**  
- Hedge funds optimize for **maximum risk-adjusted return**, not maximum raw return  
- Example: The Medallion Fund’s early returns (~15% in 1988-89) may seem modest but were achieved with **significantly lower risk** compared to market benchmarks  
- Hedge funds withstand **black swan events** through diversification and risk limits (e.g., geopolitical shocks like the Middle East conflict)  
- Models have **liquidity constraints**—scalping models cannot handle large capital due to slippage  

---

### Prop Firm Risk Management

**Goal:**  
- Achieve a **high pass rate** and **high probability of payout** within prop firm rules  
- Optimize for **low variance risk geometry** rather than high risk-reward or low win rate "sniper entries" often promoted on social media  

**Risk Geometry Insights:**  
- High win rate strategies (60–70%+) with **moderate risk per trade** and risk-reward ratios between **1:0.75 and 1:1.5** yield better survival chances  
- High variance strategies produce large but inconsistent profits and risk **account blowup** due to consecutive losses  
- Prop firm trading is essentially trading a **synthetic market defined by their rules** rather than the real market  
- Risk profile changes across phases:  
  - **Evaluation phase:** low variance, high win rate to pass challenge  
  - **Funded phase:** optimize for higher outcome/payout  

**Additional Considerations:**  
- Prop firms carry regulatory and operational risks; withdrawals should be prioritized over compounding on the prop account  
- The presenter’s experience with a Canadian CFD prop firm showed payouts can be delayed or flagged due to regulatory issues

### Competition Trading: CME Group Equity Cup and World Trading Cup

**CME Group Equity Cup:**  
- Objective: build equity quickly with **minimum downside** under **extremely limited time** and **single account** constraints  
- Cannot test multiple accounts or strategies sequentially—only one shot with the best model  
- Uses **high win rate scalping with low variance**  
- Drawdowns must remain low due to limited margin and time  
- Risk protocol is aggressive compared to hedge funds and prop firms  

**World Trading Cup:**  
- Objective: maximize **terminal rank position** with **convex upside** under **symmetric incentives** and limited time  
- Different from personal, prop, or hedge fund trading—focus on **final leaderboard rank**, not risk-adjusted returns or survivability  
- Sequential accounts allowed, but simultaneous accounts or illegal tactics (e.g., arbitrage) are forbidden  
- Strategy involves:  
  1. **Regime detection and live testing** (discard unfit models early)  
  2. **Validation and moderate risk scaling**  
  3. **Aggressive convex payoff deployment** in later stages  
- Most participants fail by being overly aggressive early, causing elimination  
- Models must incorporate **real execution costs and slippage**, especially for high-frequency strategies (500–600 trades per quarter)  
- Swing trading and small accounts are suboptimal due to insufficient sample size and high commission impact

### Personal Account Risk Management

**Goal:**  
- Achieve **risk-adjusted return**, **psychological stability**, and **long-term compounding**  
- Moderate risk tolerance allows compounding profits by increasing risk moderately after exceptional months  

**Key Protocols:**  
- Use **multiple accounts and brokers** to mitigate **tail risk** (e.g., brokerage failure like FTX)  
- Diversify by brokerage, model type (discretionary, algorithmic), and asset class (futures, crypto, options)  
- Risk per trade should be **conservative to moderate** (0.5%–1.25%) for survival and psychological comfort  
- Emphasizes **measurement and tracking** of performance for continual improvement  
- Strategy evolution and optimization are critical to avoid strategy decay  
- Example personal models include options trading, earnings surprise strategies, and blockchain intelligence for Bitcoin accumulation

### Key Insights

- **Risk management is the core differentiator between retail and professional traders.**  
- **Hedge funds focus on risk-adjusted return and survivability, not just raw profits.**  
- **Prop firms require low variance, high win rate strategies to survive their unique rules.**  
- **Competition trading demands strategic regime testing, validation, and controlled aggression timed to leaderboard dynamics.**  
- **Personal accounts benefit from diversification across brokers and moderate risk to ensure survival and psychological stability.**  
- **Ignoring real execution costs and slippage can turn theoretically profitable models into losing ones.**  
- **Trading strategies must evolve and adapt continuously; complacency leads to strategy decay.**  
- **Game theory and understanding competitor behavior are critical in competitions and prop firm environments.**

### Conclusion

The video underscores the importance of tailoring **risk management frameworks to specific trading contexts**. Whether managing hedge fund capital, navigating prop firm challenges, competing in trading tournaments, or preserving personal trading capital, **understanding risk geometry, time horizons, and incentive structures is essential for long-term success and survivability**. The presenter advocates for **measured, disciplined approaches with continuous adaptation and realistic consideration of market and execution realities**.

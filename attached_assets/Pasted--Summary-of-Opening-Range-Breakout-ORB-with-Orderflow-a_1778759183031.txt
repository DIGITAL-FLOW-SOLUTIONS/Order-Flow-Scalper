### Summary of Opening Range Breakout (ORB) with Orderflow and Statistical Enhancement

The video presents an advanced approach to the **Opening Range Breakout (ORB)** trading strategy, originally popularized by Toby Crabel in the 1990s, which involves trading the breakout of the first 15 minutes of the New York cash session. This strategy is known for its consistent profitability, positive profit factor, and sharp ratio. The presenter explains how to **enhance the ORB edge by combining it with orderflow triggers and deep statistical analysis** developed in collaboration with the Deep Charts team.

---

### Core Concepts and Methodology

- **Opening Range Breakout (ORB):**
  - The ORB defines a range from the first 15, 30, or 60 minutes of the New York session — the most liquid and highest volume period of the day.
  - The breakout of this range signals which side (buyers or sellers) won the initial battle, providing a directional bias for the day.
  - The strategy uses the **top and bottom levels** of this range as key reference points.
  
- **Market Dynamics During the New York Session:**
  - The session represents the peak volume and aggressiveness between buyers and sellers.
  - Price action near the range includes **rejection, absorption, and exhaustion** signals visible through volume and orderflow.
  - The winner of the initial battle sets the trend direction, leveraging a "wall of balance" that protects the trade.

- **Adding Orderflow Confirmation:**
  - Orderflow analysis identifies **aggressive buyers/sellers, absorption, and exhaustion** at key levels.
  - This confirmation improves entry points and risk-to-reward by validating the breakout and filtering false signals.
  - It allows traders to define **precise stop-loss points** and **dynamic profit targets**.
  
- **Volume Profile Integration:**
  - The model incorporates volume profile data to detect the **Point of Control (POC)**—the price level with the highest traded volume during the initial range.
  - The **value area** around the POC serves as a sensitive zone for potential reloading or retracement trades.
  - Breaking below the **value area low** or above the **value area high** serves as a **clear invalidation point** for the trade setup.

- **Deep Statistical Analysis & Machine Learning:**
  - The Deep Charts team applies neural networks and machine learning to historical data to predict:
    - The **highest probable price excursion** after an ORB breakout.
    - Two profit targets (TP1 and TP2) with probabilities around 65-70% for TP1.
  - This statistical model automates part of the directional and target analysis, allowing traders to focus on orderflow confirmation.

- **Mean Reversion Opportunities:**
  - Within the defined range, traders can also take **mean reverting trades** on rejections at the top or bottom of the range, supported by volume and orderflow signals.
  - This is useful during consolidation sessions when the direction is unclear.

### Definitions and Terms

| Term                         | Definition                                                                                          |
|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Opening Range Breakout (ORB)  | Trading strategy based on the breakout of the first 15–60 minutes of the New York session          |
| Initial Balance (IB)          | The established price range in early session used as a reference for breakouts                    |
| Point of Control (POC)        | Price level with the highest volume traded during a defined period                                 |
| Value Area                   | Price range around the POC where most volume occurs, serving as a high-interest zone for traders  |
| Orderflow                    | Real-time analysis of market orders showing aggression, absorption, and exhaustion of buyers/sellers |
| Protection Level             | Statistically derived target price after breakout, representing high-probability profit taking zone|
| Absorption                   | Situation where aggressive orders fail to move price, indicating strong counterparty interest     |
| Aggression                   | Presence of market participants pushing price decisively in one direction                          |
